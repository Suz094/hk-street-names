About this app

Lou Paai (路牌, "street sign") covers 4543 streets across Hong Kong's 18 districts, plus 172 MTR/Light Rail/ferry stops, 12 major attractions, and 1815 licensed hotels and guesthouses — all drawn from official public registers. Every street shows a tone-marked Jyutping reading; other categories show a plain-letter reading with the tone dropped — see "How the readings work" below.

Most streets (4189 of 4543) also show a neighbourhood name, such as Mid-Levels or Causeway Bay — assigned by finding each street's nearest labelled neighbourhood centre in OpenStreetMap data. It's the closest match, not a guarantee the whole street sits inside that neighbourhood's informal boundary.

Using it with a taxi driver: search or browse to your destination, tap it, then hold the big card up — the Chinese name is enlarged so it's easy to read at a glance.

How the readings work

Every reading is built the same way: look up the standard Cantonese pronunciation of each Chinese character individually, using Unicode's public character database. For streets, the tone is kept (marked 1–6, see the table below); for Transport, Sightseeing, and Hotels, the tone is dropped for a simpler plain-letter reading.

Either way, it's a character-by-character reading, not a verified phrase-level pronunciation — it's enough to sound a name out loud, but won't always match how it's said in fluent, connected speech. A smaller set of well-known streets additionally get a hand-checked line of history.

The speaker icon next to a reading plays the Chinese name aloud using your device's own text-to-speech voice. Quality and availability depend on what Cantonese voice (if any) is installed on your phone — some devices will fall back to Mandarin or another Chinese voice instead.

Two kinds of sign

Most Hong Kong street signs today are the Transport Department's dark green enamel plates, English above Chinese, both centred in white — the style this app borrows.

In older quarters like Sheung Wan and Central you'll still spot colonial-era ceramic tile signs: cream tiles, black English lettering, and Chinese characters glazed in red.
