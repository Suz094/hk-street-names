About this app

Lou Paai (路牌, "street sign") covers all 4543 officially named streets across Hong Kong's 18 districts, drawn from the government's public street name register. Every entry includes a plain-letter reading alongside the Chinese name — see "How the readings work" below.

Using it with a taxi driver: search or browse to your destination, tap it, then hold the big card up — the Chinese name is enlarged so it's easy to read at a glance.

How the readings work

Every street's card shows a plain-letter reading built by looking up the standard Cantonese pronunciation of each Chinese character individually, using Unicode's public character database, then dropping the tone. It's enough to sound a name out loud — it won't always match how a phrase is said in fluent, connected speech.

For a smaller set of well-known streets, the card instead shows a hand-checked Jyutping reading with tone numbers (1–6, marking pitch) plus a line of history.

Two kinds of sign

Most Hong Kong street signs today are the Transport Department's dark green enamel plates, English above Chinese, both centred in white — the style this app borrows.

In older quarters like Sheung Wan and Central you'll still spot colonial-era ceramic tile signs: cream tiles, black English lettering, and Chinese characters glazed in red.
