About this app

Lou Paai (路牌, "street sign") covers 4543 streets across Hong Kong's 18 districts, plus 172 MTR/Light Rail/ferry stops, 11 major attractions, and 1815 licensed hotels and guesthouses — all drawn from official public registers. Every entry includes a plain-letter reading alongside the Chinese name — see "How the readings work" below.

Most streets (4189 of 4543) also show a neighbourhood name, such as Mid-Levels or Causeway Bay — assigned by finding each street's nearest labelled neighbourhood centre in OpenStreetMap data. It's the closest match, not a guarantee the whole street sits inside that neighbourhood's informal boundary.

Using it with a taxi driver: search or browse to your destination, tap it, then hold the big card up — the Chinese name is enlarged so it's easy to read at a glance.

How the readings work

Every card shows a plain-letter reading built by looking up the standard Cantonese pronunciation of each Chinese character individually, using Unicode's public character database, then dropping the tone. It's enough to sound a name out loud — it won't always match how a phrase is said in fluent, connected speech.

For a smaller set of well-known streets, the card instead shows a hand-checked Jyutping reading with tone numbers (1–6, marking pitch) plus a line of history.

Two kinds of sign

Most Hong Kong street signs today are the Transport Department's dark green enamel plates, English above Chinese, both centred in white — the style this app borrows.

In older quarters like Sheung Wan and Central you'll still spot colonial-era ceramic tile signs: cream tiles, black English lettering, and Chinese characters glazed in red.
